# Product vision

*573 words of body text; 603 including the section headings.*

## What I noticed

Most products that added memory recently try to learn *the person* — tone,
temperament, interests — to make the assistant feel closer to you. Kivi is in a rarer
position. It doesn't need to infer who you are, because it already has the thing that
matters: everything you said, when you said it, and where it went.

A chat assistant knows what you asked it. Kivi knows what you *told other people* —
the deadline you gave your team, the promise you made to a partner. That is the value:
not a personality profile, a record of your work in your words.

## What semantic memory should let Kivi become

The thing you ask instead of searching six apps. Everything you dictate has already
scattered into Slack, Gmail, Linear, a Notion doc you will not find again. Kivi saw
all of it leave. It should be the one place that can answer "what did we decide about
UPI autopay, and why" without you knowing where to look.

## Where the value actually is

Three things, and I would cut anything that is not one of them:

1. **Recovering what you said** by time, app and subject — "the update I sent around
   5pm yesterday". This alone justifies the feature.
2. **Assembling a fact you never stated in one place.** The Truvia failure took three
   dictations over three weeks to become a story. No single message contains it.
3. **Producing work that sounds like you.** You have already said how you want things
   written. Kivi should not need to be told twice.

## What deserves to be remembered

Only what you said, in three shapes: **facts** about your work that stay true for weeks
(dates, owners, decisions, numbers); **preferences** about how you want language
produced, but only when you stated them as a rule; and **episodes** — one line per
dictation, so anything you ever said stays findable.

Everything else is thrown away on purpose. Moods, jokes and the transient content of
one message are noise a week later. And anything about your health, your money, your
family, your politics, or a password you dictated to yourself is deliberately
unlearnable — not filtered at read time, never written down at all. Kivi hears those
things because it hears everything. It should be the product that proves it did not
keep them.

## What Kivi must never assume

That it understood you. Two failures matter above the rest: answering with a fact that
has since changed, and answering at all when your history does not contain the answer.
So a memory exists only if Kivi can quote the sentence that created it; a statement
that contradicts an older one replaces it and keeps the old one as history; and if
retrieval comes back empty, Kivi says so. "You never told me that" is a correct answer.

## Why anyone would trust it

Because a normal person can check it in a minute. Every memory shows the sentence that
produced it and the date. Every answer shows what it was built from. One tap deletes a
memory, and the same tap works mid-conversation when Kivi gets something wrong. No
settings to administer, no consent dialogue — just a page that says, in your own words,
what Kivi thinks it knows.

## What this rules out

Dictation stays as it is: a fast, predictable path from speech to text. Letting memory
reach into it would make the same sentence come out differently on different days.
Semantic memory earns its place in Hey Kivi, where you asked a question and can see the
reasoning — and nowhere else.
